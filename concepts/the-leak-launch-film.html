The idea

Every painting company in the country is bleeding money through channels it doesn't control, and calling that bleed "marketing." So the film opens in a world that has been drained — a blacked-out warehouse where forty paint buckets, each stenciled with a channel name, pour grey onto the floor. No color anywhere. That's the business before.

The payoff is literal for a painting company: the agents come online and color returns to the world. They seal the holes, chroma races up the walls, and the last thing the viewer sees is a fully saturated room and a man who built the thing himself. Nobody in the contractor-coaching space is making this. That's the point.

Four acts, one 15-second generation each. You'll batch four takes per act, keep the strongest, and cut a 60-second film out of four clips.

Before you generate

Lock the character first

Shoot four reference stills of yourself before you touch Higgsfield: black work jacket, same haircut, hard side light against a dark wall, one straight-on, one 3/4, one profile, one full body. Feed those as the character reference or start frame on every act you appear in. Without it, act one and act four look like two different people and the whole film falls apart.

Wardrobe never changes

One outfit for the entire film: black work jacket, dark tee, dark work pants, boots. The only thing that changes across 60 seconds is the color of the world around you. If the jacket changes, the metaphor breaks.

One camera move per clip

15 seconds is long for a generation. Give each clip a single dominant move that runs the whole duration, and write the action as one continuous take. The phrase "continuous shot, no cuts" is in every prompt on purpose. Asking for cuts inside one generation is what produces morphing.

Batch strategy

Your four slots per act aren't four rolls of the same dice. Take A is the hero. B, C and D are genuinely different stagings, so if the hero fails you still have a usable act instead of four broken versions of the same idea.

Sound & post

Sound design

ACT I — sub drone at 40Hz, thick liquid pour, distant warehouse room tone. No music yet. Silence is the hook.

ACT II — paper storm, one reverse riser building the whole act, everything drops out for a half-second before the ember.

ACT III — the drop lands exactly on the palm press. Six ignition hits, one per agent. Big low-end.

ACT IV — music pulls back to a single sustained pad so his voice sits on top. Never talk over a drop.

Cutting four clips into sixty seconds

Each act ends on a held frame and the next begins on movement, so straight cuts work — no transitions, no dissolves, no whip wipes. The only exception is Act II into Act III: hard cut to two frames of black on the ember, then the palm press. That black is the whole edit's punctuation.

If a generation drifts in the last two seconds, trim it and let the act run 13 seconds. A 56-second film is fine. A morphing face is not.

Text treatment

Never burn text into the generation — it comes out garbled. Add supers in post. Condensed uppercase, bottom third, appearing on a beat and cutting out hard rather than fading. Two lines maximum. Every number gets its own screen.

The thumbnail

Pull it from Act III Take C — him at the center of the frozen color explosion. That single frame is the most arresting image in the film and it works at 40 pixels wide in a feed, which is the only size that matters.

One caution before you post

The $47,915 and $124,000 are your real numbers and they're the strongest thing in this — but the second you put revenue on screen you invite screenshots. Make sure you're comfortable with those figures living publicly and permanently, because they will. If you'd rather not, Act III still lands hard on "six agents, one weekend" with no dollar figure at all.

Why "comment AGENT" beats "send me a DM"

Comments feed the algorithm, DMs don't. Ask for the comment publicly, then DM everyone who leaves one. You get the reach and the conversation. Put the DM ask in the video's end card and the comment ask in the caption so they're not competing.
